# 成人刀剑分层身体 v1：首轮生成提示

Status: WIP, independent art sample. Not registered in presentation.json or Main.
Tool: built-in image_gen.imagegen; transparent_background=true.
Reference role: project swordsman.png is a painting/style reference only, not an edit target and not a face to duplicate.

Use case: stylized-concept.
Asset type: a production-ready transparent sprite atlas for a limited 2D cutout adult swordsman body in a late-Han Chinese game.
Primary request: paint one coherent set of detached, fully painted body pieces, all belonging to the same anonymous adult with medium build, limited three-quarter view facing screen right. Match the supplied reference's fine hand-painted semi-realistic ink contours, muted olive linen, warm cream trousers, brown cloth belt, charcoal leg wraps and brown cloth shoes. Clear brushwork, restrained shading, cloth weight and believable adult proportions. No heroic armor, no ornament, no modern clothes. Neutral low-detail head with a simple tied topknot, understated facial planes, no distinctive eyes, scars or moustache; this is a shared battle body, not a new character portrait. Gentle consistent lighting from upper front-left on all parts.

Composition: square 1536x1536 atlas, 4 columns and 4 rows of equal invisible 384x384 cells. Each cell contains only its named detached part centered with wide transparent gutters. Do not draw the grid, labels, numbers, frames, guide lines, a background, shadows outside parts, or a complete assembled character. Sixteen cells in reading order:
Row 1: (1) head with attached neck, facing right, plain dark tied hair; (2) sleeveless torso including the complete crossed olive collar, brown waist sash and split short tunic skirt, from shoulders to mid-thigh, fully painted underneath sleeves, no head or arms; (3) near upper arm with loose olive sleeve, shoulder to elbow; (4) far upper arm with same olive sleeve, shoulder to elbow.
Row 2: (5) near forearm, sleeve cuff and bare forearm, elbow to wrist; (6) far forearm, same cuff and forearm; (7) near closed gripping hand, thumb and fingers around an empty thin sword grip opening, with a short wrist extension; (8) far relaxed guarding hand, fingers naturally together, with a short wrist extension.
Row 3: (9) near upper leg in loose cream trousers, hip to knee; (10) far upper leg in same trousers, hip to knee; (11) near lower leg with dark cloth shin wraps, knee to ankle; (12) far lower leg in the same wraps, knee to ankle.
Row 4: (13) near brown soft cloth shoe with short ankle extension, toe facing screen right; (14) far brown soft cloth shoe with short ankle extension, same three-quarter direction; (15) one separate plain straight single-handed Chinese sword, blade pointing upward, simple small bronze guard and dark wrapped hilt, no hand attached; (16) completely empty and transparent.

All limbs point approximately down in their cells in a relaxed, nearly straight rest pose; no extreme foreshortening. The sword alone points upward. Both sides are separate and anatomically compatible, with the far side slightly narrower from the same three-quarter perspective, not resized at random. Keep the parts at one common drawing scale: assembled adult would be about 900 pixels tall and 7 to 7.5 anatomical heads tall; torso including skirt about 315 pixels high, anatomical head about 115 pixels high plus neck and topknot, upper arm about 175 pixels long, forearm about 160, hands about 65, upper leg about 240, lower leg about 225, shoes about 100 long. The sword is drawn at half the body drawing scale so the whole weapon fits its cell; it will be uniformly scaled up in the rig.

Animation construction: every shoulder/elbow/hip/knee end must be a generous softly rounded painted overlap cap extending roughly 20% beyond its eventual joint center. Fill hidden cloth surfaces completely so rotating the next part does not expose white or transparency at the joint. Neck extends well below jaw into the collar; wrists extend into cuffs; ankles extend into shoes. No disconnected fragments within a piece. No cutaway anatomy, bones, blood or injury. Garment hem and sash belong to torso as one shape. The output must have real transparent alpha, including every gutter and the entire empty last cell; do not paint white or checkerboard pixels as a background. Preserve hand-painted edges without a white halo.

Constraints: all visible artwork belongs to exactly those fifteen separate parts; no additional assembled figure, alternate poses, extra weapons, text, arrows, borders or baked drop shadows. This is a functional cutout atlas, not a diagram or a poster. No facial identity replacement. No wardrobe interchangeability is claimed.
